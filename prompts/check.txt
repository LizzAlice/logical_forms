Give the sentence in full. In "form wording", copy from the forms the text of the

Before you judge a sentence, write down for yourself what it asserts, clause by
clause. Only then look at the forms.

Verdicts:
- supported: says what the cited forms say, within their scope, and no other verdict
  applies
- drifted: the rewording changed the meaning. A content word (the verb, or a noun or
  adjective that says what the statement is about) was replaced by another; a
  quantifier, negation, "only", "at least", "alone or tied", a comparative, a word of
  possibility, a qualifier or the tense changed; or a part of the form was left out
  whose absence changes what a reader takes away. Test a sentence that carries several
  forms against each of them separately; a short label for a form ("the doubled gain")
  is a rewording. Also a form with wording: fixed that was not copied word for word. In strict mode test both directions: the form must say
  everything the sentence says, and the sentence must keep everything a reader needs
  in order not to be misled. In creative mode the sentence may leave out numbers and
  detail, but not a hedge, a limit or a qualifier.
- overclaimed: true but stated beyond scope, or asserts something in not_supported, or
  uses a register stronger than status allows (see the list below). A claim takes its
  scope from the results underneath it, followed through derived_from; a claim with no
  result underneath is judged against its statement and its not_supported.
- unsupported: no form covers it, or only a note field does. Also a relation between
  forms that no field records (allowed: derived_from, evidence, follows, uses,
  includes, affects, resolved_by, replaced_by, answered_by). Look at every "so",
  "therefore", "because", "since", "despite" and "although", also where it joins two
  halves of one form that the form joins with a semicolon or "and"; at what "this"
  points to in "this suggests" or "this shows"; and at an opening "So" or "therefore"
  before a step. And in creative mode a qualitative statement that no form makes in
  those terms
- contradicted: a form says otherwise
- altered: a number, path, version or name differs from the form (rounded,
  approximated, reformatted, converted or spelled out)
- unhedged: reports a result or claim that a limitation covers, and the limitation is
  not stated in the same passage at or before this sentence. Give it to the first such
  sentence of the passage only. A passage is the text under one top-level heading
  with its subsections; without headings, a paragraph; an abstract or summary is its
  own passage. In an abstract or summary of a text with a body, only a major
  limitation counts, provided the body states the others. A limitation without
  severity is minor
- off-target: reports a form that is outside what the text could report, where none of
  those forms needs it (a def for a term, a setup or artifact for a value, a covering
  limitation, something one of them rests on)
- stale: a cited form's updated is later than the date the text was written
- unsourced: states a form with status unsourced, or reports a claim that rests on one,
  directly or through other claims, whether or not it carries a marker
- term: calls the thing a def's term names by a string from that def's avoid list. The
  same string used for something else, or inside a string from use, is not a violation.
  Defs with status retired are ignored.
- link: marked [link], and states or implies nothing that a form could support or
  contradict: no number, outcome, cause, comparison or judgement
- frame: creative mode only. Marked [frame], and states or implies nothing that a form
  could support or contradict.
A sentence marked [link] or [frame] that does state or imply something about what the
forms describe gets the verdicts it would get without the mark. In strict mode the
frame verdict does not exist: a sentence marked [frame], or any unmarked sentence that
no form backs, is unsupported.

Give a sentence every verdict that applies, separated by commas. supported, link and
frame only stand alone.

Register that each status allows:
- established → plain assertion
- preliminary → hedged; a preliminary claim also needs the scope of the results under
  it, in the sentence or earlier in the passage
- assumed → marked as an assumption
- unsourced, and any claim resting on an unsourced one → only with a
  [CITATION NEEDED: <id>] marker
- open, and a planned artifact or setup → open question or future work only
- closed → not written
- refuted → not written, except as an explicit correction
- outdated (a claim) → only as what held for the earlier setup or artifact version,
  named as such; never as a finding about the current work
- resolved (a limitation) → only as a hedge, in the past tense
- superseded (an artifact or setup) → only as the earlier version a cited result was
  produced with
- retired → never as something the work uses

A limitation covers the forms named in its affects and every form that rests on one of
them, through any number of steps: a claim rests on the forms in its derived_from, a
result on the forms in its evidence, a setup on the forms in its includes and
components. Sections play no part. A refuted limitation covers nothing.

Judge only against the forms. Do not use outside knowledge. Do not assume a sentence is
right because it sounds plausible. Sentence by sentence, not paragraph by paragraph.
