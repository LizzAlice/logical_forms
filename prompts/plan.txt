In evidence, a value that does not start with P-, DEF-, ART-, SET- or SEC- is not a form
id: it is a run label, or the entry stated, which marks a limitation that rests on the
author's own knowledge.

Targets: <section ids or form ids>
Text to write: <a methods section, a summary for a general audience, …>
Mode: strict
Lead with: <form ids, or leave empty>

Length: <words for the abstract or the whole text, or leave empty>

The mode decides only whether invented sentences, stand-ins and gist are allowed.

Do not write the text yet. Return a plan:
- the headings of the text as a tree, sections with their subsections, laid out the
  way this kind of text is normally laid out. A paper on several pieces of work gets
  one section per piece, each with the subsections a reader expects (method, results,
  and where there is something to say, limitations and interpretation);
- for each heading: one line saying what the part is to get across, and the ids of
  the forms it reports, in the order they will appear. Results parts open with a
  claim where there is one;
- the tables: parallel things (stages, models with the same settings, the same
  measurement for several runs) go in a table. For each table: its rows, its columns
  and the forms that fill it;
- for each top-level section, the limitations that cover a form it reports and the
  assumptions that a claim in it rests on, and where they will be stated;
- for an abstract: its length (at most 200 words unless Length says otherwise) and
  the few forms it reports, which are the Lead with forms;
- the targets you leave out and those you only summarise, each with a reason.

You may leave out a target. You may not leave out a limitation that covers a form the
text reports, or an assumption that a reported claim rests on, unless it has
surface: no. Every line of the plan names forms; the plan adds no content of its own.
A form outside the targets may appear only where a target needs it: a def for a term,
a setup or artifact for a value, a limitation that covers a target, an assumption or
another form that a target rests on.

A limitation covers the forms named in its affects and every form that rests on one of
them, through any number of steps: a claim rests on the forms in its derived_from, a
result on the forms in its evidence, a setup on the forms in its includes and
components. Sections play no part. A refuted limitation covers nothing. An abstract or
summary of a text with a body must state every major limitation that covers something
it reports; a minor one may be left out there if the body states it. A limitation
without severity is minor.
